Ranking Refresh-Ready Content

Ahmed El-Dasoky · lane: Refresh / Content Opportunity Scoring · 2026

Abstract. Which pages should a content team review for refresh, in what order, when they can only read ~50 per pass? Working on the bundled 30,000-page anonymized 90-day snapshot, I frame the lane as ranking, build a transparent hand-rule baseline first, and train logistic regression and random-forest models on a client-grouped holdout. Random forest reaches ROC-AUC 0.750 and Precision@50 0.74 (baseline 0.627 / 0.24); the winning signals are backward-looking visibility, position, and freshness. The output is a ranked, human-reviewable refresh queue with reason codes — decision support for triage.

1. Problem & decision

A content operator manages hundreds of pages and must decide which to review this week. A wrong call costs a human's review time or, worse, lets a declining, high-demand page rot. Because 16,262 of 30,000 pages (54%) are flagged declining, triage truly helps: rank the pool so the highest-leverage pages surface first.

2. Data & safety

Bundled content_refresh_anonymized.csv (30,000 pages × 44 columns, fixed 90-day snapshot). Excluded: provider_used, model_used (internal provenance, not outcome), trend_direction (defines the label), raw IDs (grouping only). Leakage audit: no label-derived features, no future windows, no product flags.

3. Baseline

Transparent hand rule: +4 declining-with-demand, +2 low CTR (visible), +2 low engagement (visible), −1 stale, scaled to 0–100, ranked descending, with reason codes.

baseline on client holdout: ROC-AUC 0.627 · avg-precision 0.468 · Precision@50 0.24 · Precision@100 0.36 · recall 0.19

4. Method

Framed as ranking/scoring. Target proxy: is_declining_label = trend_direction=="down". Features: 16 backward-looking aggregates (impressions/clicks/ sessions/engagement 90d, position, CTR, freshness, word/char counts), heavy tails log-transformed. Models: logistic regression, decision tree, random forest (random_state=42).

5. Validation

Client-grouped holdout (GroupShuffleSplit, 20% of clients, seed 42) — a client's pages never straddle train/test, mirroring a mixed-client production queue. Same data, metric, split as the baseline.

6. What the model leans on

Top features: days_with_impressions (0.16), log_impressions_90d (0.13), avg_position (0.11), content_age_days (0.10). Interpretation: a page that is persistently visible, aging, and under-clicking is the highest-leverage refresh. Surprise: search_volume / competition rank low once observed impressions and position are present — keyword demand is already folded into visibility.

7. Ranked recommendation

refresh → refresh_and_review_ctr → refresh_and_review_engagement → monitor

A FlyRank editor reads the top 20 of the queue, confirms demand (search_volume, competition) and that the page is actually underperforming, then acts. Confidence: high for triage; none for "will recover" — this work scores decline-risk and routes review.

8. Error analysis & limits

False positives are recently-updated, high-impression pages whose labels flipped down for demand reasons — acceptable for triage, the model reads "visible + engaged" as healthy even when momentum fades. Limits: 90-day snapshot, shipped rule as label, no recovery prediction, no page text or competitor signals, 30,000 pages / 44 columns only.
